MCDA Climate Risk Score Explanation

Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA) grounded in empirical NASA & European Earth Observations

COMPOSITE CLIMATE RESILIENCE FORMULATION (0 – 100)
Rcomposite = 0.35 × Thermal Hazard + 0.25 × Vegetation Deficit + 0.20 × Urban Density + 0.20 × Population Exposure

Where each dimension is normalized from satellite-derived physical observables (0 = optimal resilience, 100 = extreme vulnerability).

35% WEIGHT

Thermal Hazard (35%)

Sensor: Landsat 8/9 TIRS Band 10 (10.895 µm)

Calculates surface thermal stress above physiological thresholds. Temperatures ≤30.0°C score 0 pts; temperatures ≥38.5°C hit maximum 100 pts.

H = min(100, max(0, ((LST − 30.0) / 8.5) × 100))
25% WEIGHT

Vegetation Deficit (25%)

Sensor: Copernicus Sentinel-2 MSI Level-2A (10m)

Quantifies urban vegetative deficit relative to the World Health Organization (WHO) minimum standard of 9.0 m² of accessible green space per capita.

V = min(100, max(0, ((0.65 − NDVI) / 0.55) × 100))
20% WEIGHT

Urban Density (20%)

Sensor: Sentinel-2 & Landsat Decadal Classification

Measures impermeable surface fraction (corrugated tin roofs, concrete, asphalt) that prevents soil infiltration and absorbs daytime solar radiation.

D = Built-Up Percentage (0 – 100%)
20% WEIGHT

Population Exposure (20%)

Dataset: WorldPop 100m Gridded Density Index

Normalizes residential and commercial population exposure to thermal hazard. Sectors with >35,000 residents/km² reach maximum vulnerability weighting.

P = min(100, (Population Density / 35,000) × 100)

Provides regional climatological validation baselines across continuous decadal time-series (2015–2026).

Gridded residential population exposure derived from census microdata, building footprints, and machine-learning dasymetric mapping.

SURF: Satellite Urban Resilience Framework

Translating NASA & European Earth Observations into Actionable Climate Resilience for Coastal River Deltas

CHAPTER 01

The Delta Crisis: Ground Zero in Southwest Bangladesh

The Ganges-Brahmaputra-Meghna delta is one of the most ecologically fragile and climate-vulnerable landscapes on Earth. Khulna, the divisional metropolis of southwestern Bangladesh, rests on the tidal banks of the Bhairab and Rupsha rivers at an average elevation of merely 3.5 meters above sea level.

Over the past decade (2015–2026), accelerated migration driven by coastal sea-level rise and cyclone displacement has triggered unzoned urban infill. Vegetated wetlands and historic tidal retention khals (canals) have been filled to accommodate low-cost settlements constructed from corrugated galvanized iron (tin) roofs and dense masonry.

CHAPTER 02

The Microclimate Amplification: Heat Islands & Canopy Loss

Unlike temperate cities with insulated roofing, tropical deltaic informal settlements rely heavily on corrugated iron sheeting with very high solar absorptance and low thermal mass. Satellite radiometry reveals a stark reality:

+4.9°C Decadal LST Increase (2015–2026)
−28.4% Net Canopy Depletion
+100% Impervious Surface Expansion
1.8°C River Cooling Buffer Effect

During dry season pre-monsoon months (April–June), daytime Land Surface Temperatures in Khulna Sadar and Khalishpur regularly surge beyond 38°C, creating dangerous physiological thermal strain for vulnerable outdoor laborers and dense informal communities.

CHAPTER 03

The Earth Observation Solution: Multi-Sensor Grounding

Municipal urban planning in developing nations has historically operated blind to microclimate physics due to lack of ground station density. SURF bridges this gap by unifying open Earth Observation data streams into a single decision-support platform:

  • Landsat 8/9 TIRS (Band 10): Radiometric calibration and Sobrino NDVI threshold emissivity modeling delivers 30m Land Surface Temperature.
  • Copernicus Sentinel-2 MSI: 10m spatial resolution Red & NIR bands provide Fractional Vegetation Cover ($P_v$) and canopy deficit analysis.
  • Terra/Aqua MODIS: Validates 8-day composite climatological thermal baselines across 11 continuous years.
  • Transparent Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA): Eliminates arbitrary scoring by weighting Thermal Hazard (35%), Canopy Deficit (25%), Impervious Density (20%), and Gridded Population Exposure (20%).
CHAPTER 04

Actionable Interventions for Municipal Planners

SURF does not merely diagnose problems; it computes targeted, satellite-grounded solutions for municipal authorities like Khulna City Corporation (KCC):

High-Albedo Cool Roof Retrofits

Applying elastomeric reflective white coatings to corrugated tin roofs increases surface albedo from 0.15 to >0.75, modeled to drop indoor temperatures by up to 3.5°C and sector LST by 1.8°C.

Linear Canopy Bioswales

Targeting barren transportation spines and riverfronts with native mangrove-adjacent trees restores evaporative cooling corridors where NDVI is currently below 0.15.

Tidal Canal Retention Buffers

Enforcing 50-meter buffer protection around Mayur and Bhairab tidal channels preserves convective breeze corridors that naturally cool adjacent sectors.
